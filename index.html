Requirement Analysis

I started my career in HR because I've always been drawn to work that solves people's problems. Understanding what frustrates people, what motivates them, and what makes their days easier — that was never just a job function for me. It was instinct.

But there was always another side to me. Growing up, I'd lose myself in painting, decorating, arranging — anything visual or creative. I didn't just want things to look good. I wanted them to feel right. I chased perfection in every detail, often without knowing why.

At some point, I felt a gap. My work was meaningful, but something was missing. I caught myself imagining what it would feel like to wake up every morning and do the thing I'd loved since childhood — not as a hobby, but as a career. That thought pushed me to explore UI/UX design.

The moment I started learning, I couldn't think of anything else. Design connected everything — my curiosity about people, my obsession with getting details right, my need for work that felt effortless and purposeful at once. I didn't just find a new skill. I found the work I was always meant to do.

Today I get to solve people's problems through design every single day. It's the perfect collision of everything I care about — and that's not something I take lightly.

How I Work

My design process.

01
Discovery & Intake
I start by understanding the real problem — mapping business goals, users, and constraints before any wireframes. My structured intake framework cuts requirement clarification by 40%.
02
User Flow Design
User journeys come first—mapping who does what, when, and why. For role-based systems, flows shape the entire product.
03
Wireframe & Review
I build wireframes iteratively and bring clients in early. Each review round is structured — feedback is documented, prioritised. Clear decisions, fast execution.
04
High-Fidelity UI
Once flows and wireframes are aligned, I move to high-fidelity design built on a scalable component system. Every screen follows the design system — consistent tokens, typography, and spacing — so the product feels unified, not assembled.
05
Client Sign-off
Before handoff, I walk the client through the final designs — not just to show screens, but to confirm every design decision aligns with the business goal we started with.
06
Dev Handoff & Build
I stay involved through the build—answering dev questions, reviewing output, and ensuring what ships matches the design. Pixel accuracy isn’t optional. I align UX with technical constraints to ship faster with less rework.
